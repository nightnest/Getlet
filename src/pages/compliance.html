Landlord compliance in England

A short guide to what applies now and what is coming. General information only.

Last reviewed: {{reviewed}}

Check current GOV.UK guidance. Rules change and phased measures have dates that can move. This page is general information, not legal advice, and covers England only. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own rules.

How GETLET fits in: GETLET coordinates gas and electrical certificates through specialist partner agencies. Arranging a certificate does not remove the landlord's legal duty. GETLET does not carry out certified work itself, and this page does not claim any scheme membership.

In force now

In force

Renters' Rights Act

The main tenancy reforms took effect in England on 1 May 2026. Section 21 was abolished for the main private regime, tenancies became assured periodic tenancies, and the section 8 possession grounds were revised. Check transitional cases rather than assuming every older notice has lapsed.

GOV.UK: implementation roadmap

In force

Gas safety

Where there is a gas appliance, an annual gas safety check by a Gas Safe registered engineer is needed. A record must be given to new tenants before they move in and to existing tenants within 28 days of the check.

HSE: gas safety for landlords

In force

Electrical safety

The electrical installation must be inspected and tested by a qualified person at least every five years, or sooner if the report says so. Required remedial work must be done within 28 days, or sooner if the report requires.

GOV.UK: electrical safety standards

In force

Energy performance (EPC)

Most privately rented homes in England must have an EPC rating of at least E, or a valid registered exemption.

GOV.UK: minimum energy efficiency standard

In force

Deposits and tenant payments

Covered deposits must be protected in an approved scheme and the required information given to the tenant within 30 days. Deposits and holding deposits are capped, and only permitted payments can be charged to tenants.

GOV.UK: deposit protection · GOV.UK: tenancy fees

In force

Licensing

Whether a property needs a licence depends on the type of property and on the local council. Large houses in multiple occupation are licensed nationally, while additional and selective licensing schemes vary by council. Check with the property's council before letting.

GOV.UK: HMO licence

Announced and phased

Announced

Landlord registration

A landlord registration service has been announced to start with a regional rollout from 15 December 2026, with all actively letting landlords expected to be registered by 14 November 2027. This is announced and phased, so check regional commencement and instructions.

GOV.UK announcement

Expected 2028

Landlord ombudsman

A landlord ombudsman is expected in 2028. It is not operating now.

Policy direction

Future energy and housing standards

Government has set out an intention to require a higher energy standard (equivalent to EPC C under reformed measures) by 1 October 2030, and a new Decent Homes Standard in 2035. These still need regulations, so they are not current requirements.

GOV.UK: energy performance response
